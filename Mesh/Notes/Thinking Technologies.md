# Thinking Technologies

A thinking technology is any system that helps a conscious mind interact with information to build models of reality. It can be physical, biological, or social. It is defined by its function in the cognitive loop, not by its hardware.

## Four domains of technology

NUU Cognition describes the history of technology as a series of domains. Each domain solves one category of problem.

| Domain | What it does | Problem it solves |
|---|---|---|
| Physical | Manipulates matter | Survival |
| Mechanical | Automates force | Labour |
| Digital | Processes logic | Calculation and transmission |
| Cognitive | Engages with information and cognition | The limit of how much complexity a mind can process |

Thinking technologies are the tools of the fourth domain.

## Thinking technologies are not new

Writing let a mind store memory outside the brain. The abacus let a mind handle numbers through touch. Calculators, spreadsheets, and note tools such as Anki and Obsidian continued this line. These tools were passive. The biological brain did all the processing. The tool was a store or a rigid guide.

## What changed

Large language models can now engage with the content of a thought, not only its container. For the first time, software can interact with information semantically. NUU Cognition holds that this crosses a threshold. It is now worth treating thinking technologies as a domain of its own, and worth building tools for cognition itself.

## Three layers

A thinking technology is a bridge between three layers of reality.

- **Physical.** It uses energy and hardware to make a tangible interface.
- **Biological.** It fits the architecture of the human brain: pattern matching, limited working memory, and a need for visual and spatial orientation.
- **Social.** Thought is rarely solitary. Ideas spread between minds. A thinking technology helps minds synchronise their models.

## Why the digital tools did not fit

Classical software was built on deterministic logic: rigid hierarchies, binary storage, and fixed sequences. Human cognition is probabilistic, associative, and semantic. We do not think in file directories. We think in webs of meaning. When thought is forced into rigid containers, the machine wins and the mind loses. NUU Cognition builds tools that start from how the mind works. See [[Models and Information Environments]].
